A miniature metalens brings diamond quantum sensing into confined spaces

← All news

In collaboration with Professor Kun Huang’s group, the laboratory developed a miniature diamond quantum sensor using a metalens. The work, “A Miniaturized Metalens-Based Spin Defect Quantum Sensor,” was published in Advanced Functional Materials.

The 600 nm-thick optical structure focuses 532 nm excitation light and collects fluorescence around 700 nm in a single planar element. It replaces the bulky microscope objective commonly used for NV-center excitation and readout.

The device supports stable fluorescence detection and spin control, with spatial resolution comparable to a conventional optical system. Experiments demonstrated spin-resonance measurements in the narrow gap of an electromagnet and high-resolution imaging of localized microwave fields.

Han-Xiang Zang and Rui-Xing Xia are the first authors; Kun Huang and Xiang-Dong Chen are the corresponding authors. The result offers a route toward compact quantum sensors for confined environments.
